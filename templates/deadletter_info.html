Deadletter.info

Pirate mail for wandering letters.

What this is

Deadletter Mail highlights the analog connection and relationship between people. Letters are written by strangers and carried hand-to-hand through the world until they reach the person they're meant for. No postal service, no street address. Finding people where they're exposed in the network.

Pirate mail addresses

A pirate mail address isn't a street, or a usps address. It's a social place or community where someone can be found; a bar, a cafe, a camp, a venue, a polycule, a volunteer group. A letter reaches its person through their external facing community.

How a letter travels

Letters travel by hand. From person to person, one stop closer at a time. An individual isn't tasked with delivery, only as many hops of distance as they can manage.

Coming Soon: Letters will carry a physical stamp with a unique number and a QR code. Anyone who finds the letter can scan the QR and log what happened — spotted it, picked it up, left it somewhere, delivered it — and add a photo or tag it. The letter's journey is digitalized as it moves.

Rules of engagement

Can't do it wrong if there's no way to do it right.

  1. Your envelope gets a topic on the back and a pirate mail address on the front — a public-ish place you're part of, not your home address. A bar, a band, a camp, a club, a family, a group, a karaoke night…
  2. Write a letter, if you feel like it, using the "write about" on the back of the envelope. Only if you're so inspired.
  3. Look through the outbound mail and take any letters for groups, people, or places you have any proximity to or familiarity with. Heading somewhere? Pass along the letters bound for that direction.

New accounts start pending organizer approval. Event source codes provide attribution only; they do not approve an account. Participant approval does not grant administrative access.

Privacy controls are being introduced in stages. Account preferences currently save future-submission defaults; they do not change existing letters. Participant-only protection is not yet implemented across all pages, APIs, and media. Do not rely on registration or a preference setting to keep a submission private at this stage.

Website visibility settings cannot prevent people handling printed letters from reading, photographing, or sharing them.

Take part

  • Request a letter — put your name and a pirate mail address on an envelope; someone out there will write to you.
  • Write a letter — at the writing desk, for someone you'll never meet.
  • Carry a letter — pick up a stamped letter and carry it toward its destination.
  • Scan a stamp — log what happened to a letter you've found.
  • Leaderboard — couriers earn reputation for keeping letters moving.

Free to participate. The project occasionally provides screen-printed patches and stickers.
